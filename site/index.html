For students and career switchers moving into climate

Find your corner of climate.

Climate and sustainability work is too broad to search in one go. This kit walks you through the method I use with the people I advise: narrow down to a handful of subsectors, go deep on them, build relationships inside them, then apply with a story that shows you know the space.

Arjun Srihari · Career advisor and adjunct faculty, IE University · Co-founder of S2S Dynamics

How I'd use this
  • Pick a starting hypothesis of 5 to 7 subsectors. You'll drop some within 6 to 8 weeks, and that's the point. Knowing what you don't want is progress.
  • For each subsector, list 15 to 20 companies in your target geography. Track them in Teal, Excel or Notion. Company databases and investor lists (below) help you find the ones that aren't household names.
  • Read their job descriptions. Note the skills and certifications you have and the ones you're missing. That gap is your plan for the year: projects, volunteering, courses, side work.
  • Company first, job title second. Many companies doing serious climate work never put "sustainability" in the job title.

Step 2 · Ongoing

Learn the space

Learn enough to talk like an insider in your subsectors, and pick up the certifications your target job descriptions keep asking for. Let the job descriptions decide the certificates, not the other way round.

How I'd use this
  • Short, free courses are for learning the vocabulary, data sources and key players of a sector. You don't need the certificate.
  • Paid certifications only when 3 or more of your target job descriptions mention them. Reporting roles look for GRI and ESRS; finance roles look for ESG analyst credentials; almost everyone benefits from knowing Scope 1, 2 and 3 carbon accounting.
  • Turn what you learn into something you can show: a short analysis, a LinkedIn post, a project on a company you're targeting.

CSRD has changed. The EU's Omnibus package (finalised in 2026) limits mandatory CSRD reporting to companies with more than 1,000 employees and €450M turnover, from 2027. ESRS skills are still valued, but fewer companies must report, so check whether your targets are in scope before investing in a reporting certification.

Step 3 · Start in week 1, never stop

Build your network

Climate communities are where you hear the news early, learn who is hiring, and find jobs that never reach a job board. Use them to learn first. Applications come later.

How I'd use this
  • Join 2 or 3 communities, and inside them only 5 to 7 channels: one broad channel plus a few for your subsectors. More than that becomes noise.
  • Post an introduction with three parts: your background, the kind of role you're aiming for, and one specific ask.
  • When someone posts a job, message them before you apply. Say why you fit and ask for 15 minutes. That's how a cold application becomes a referral.
  • Give before you ask: share an article, an event, a quick bit of research. Then keep the relationship going.
  • At events and conferences, volunteering often gets you into the rooms where speakers and investors are.
Intro post template
Hi all, I'm [name], based in [city] after [X years in Y]. I'm exploring [subsector 1] and [subsector 2], especially [value-chain role]. I'd love to talk to anyone working on [specific thing] in [geography]. Happy to share what I'm learning in return.

Step 4 · When your list is ready

Track and apply

Use job boards to discover companies in your subsectors, and investor portfolio boards to find funded companies that are hiring. Then go through people, not just portals.

How I'd use this
  • Sort target companies into three tiers: dream companies, good fits for your current skills, and safe bets. Apply across all three.
  • Expect volume. In today's market, plan on around 100 serious applications. Easy Apply rarely works because roles fill or close within days.
  • Keep one master CV and tailor keywords to each job description. Applicant tracking systems filter on them.
  • Practise interviews with companies lower on your list before your top targets.
  • Visa sponsorship is not always a dead end. Larger companies handle it more easily, and referrals move faster than cold applications.
